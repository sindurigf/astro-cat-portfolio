I was eight when the World Government put a bounty on me. For the next twenty years, asking anyone for anything was dangerous.

## Twenty Years of Not Asking

People who took me in often turned me in for the money, or were afraid of what I would bring them. So I learned to rely on nobody. I worked for dangerous people, because they could protect me.

When I joined the Straw Hat Pirates, Usopp asked what my specialty was. I told him the truth: assassination. He was terrified, and to be fair, that was a reasonable reaction.

## The Day I Said It

When the World Government came after me again, I left the crew without explaining why. I thought that was the only way to keep them safe.

They followed me to Enies Lobby anyway. Our captain had the World Government's flag burned, which was a declaration of war. Then he told me to say what I wanted.

I had never said it out loud before. I said that I wanted to live, and to go back to sea with them. The whole island heard me.

## What I Learned

- Saying what you want out loud is scary, and it is also the only way people can help you.
- Leaving to protect people makes the decision for them. They may not agree with it.
- A crew gets to choose its own risks.

## Saying No Is Part of It

Asking for what you want also means being clear about what you do not want.

On Thriller Bark, the boys combined their bodies into one giant fighter to take on a giant zombie. They were thrilled, and they had saved a spot for me as the left arm.

I said no. As a human being, I found it embarrassing. When Franky and Chopper asked again, I asked them never to ask me to dock again. I still smile when I think about their faces.

## Two Years Later

When the crew was split up, the Revolutionary Army wanted me to meet their leader. I told them I had to get back to my captain. On the way, a newspaper arrived with news about him, and I laughed out loud.
