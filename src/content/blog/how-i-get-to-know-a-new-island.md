I have been to a lot of islands, often in a hurry. Some were deserts, one was in the sky and one was full of zombies. These are the habits that help me get to know a new place.

## 1. Find the Oldest Stone

On every island I look for the oldest thing that still stands. In Arabasta, that was the Poneglyph in the Tomb of the Kings, under the capital.

Crocodile, my employer at the time, wanted it to tell him where to find an ancient weapon. It did. I told him it only held the history of Arabasta. He never got that weapon from me.

## 2. Respect the Ruins

Shandora, on the sky island of Skypiea, was built 1,100 years ago. I know because I read the date on the city's own tombstone.

While I was there, a man started smashing the ruins while he chased me. I told him to treasure history more carefully, and then I stopped him myself. A ruin cannot be rebuilt.

## 3. Look for Who Came Before You

On Shandora's golden bell I read another Poneglyph. Next to it, someone had written a message in the same ancient script about twenty years earlier. It was Gol D. Roger, the Pirate King. I did not expect to find company that far up.

## 4. Do Not Skip the Scary Islands

When the island of Thriller Bark caught our ship, most of the crew wanted to stay on board. I went ashore with our captain, because I like a thrill.

The island was full of zombies, and its guard dog had three heads. Honestly, I found the dog cute.

## 5. Bring a Sketchbook

On the way to Fish-Man Island, our ship went thousands of meters below the surface. A kraken attacked us on the way down. It was a rare sight, so I sketched it before we dealt with it.

## 6. Ask Who Built It

Tequila Wolf is a stone bridge in the East Blue. Enslaved workers have been building it for 700 years, and for a while I was one of them. When I asked why it was being built, nobody would tell me. The Revolutionary Army freed us.

Now I ask the same two questions about every big building I see: what was it for, and who was forced to build it?

## 7. Pick the Right Season

My favorite is autumn on a spring island. The weather is mild, the ruins are dry and there is time for a good cup of coffee.
