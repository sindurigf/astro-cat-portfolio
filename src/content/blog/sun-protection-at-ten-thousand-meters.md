Skypiea is a sky island ten thousand meters above the sea. I spent a full day there exploring the ruins of Shandora, with almost no shade. Since then, I take sun protection as seriously as I take old books.

This is what I use, and why.

## Why Height and Water Matter

Ultraviolet light gets stronger the higher you go. The [EPA puts the increase at about 6% for every kilometer](https://www.epa.gov/sunsafety/learn-about-uv-index), and Skypiea is ten kilometers up.

Life at sea does not make it easier. [Water, snow and sand reflect the sun](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/skin-cancer/prevent/how), and [fresh snow can reflect up to 80% of it](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/radiation-the-known-health-effects-of-ultraviolet-radiation). On deck, the sun reaches you from below as well as from above.

A sunburn shows within hours. The damage that can lead to skin cancer builds up over years. As an archaeologist, I spend a lot of time with things that were damaged slowly. I would rather not add my own skin to that list.

## My Routine

I follow the [American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/sun-protection/shade-clothing-sunscreen/how-to-apply-sunscreen):

- **Sunscreen:** broad spectrum, water resistant, SPF 30 or higher.
- **Amount:** about one ounce for the body, which is roughly a shot glass.
- **Timing:** fifteen minutes before going outside, so it has time to work.
- **Reapplying:** every two hours, and straight after swimming or sweating.
- **Hard-to-reach spots:** the Academy suggests asking someone to help with your back. I can sprout an extra pair of hands, so I do it myself. Everyone else should ask a friend.
- **Cover:** a wide-brimmed hat and sunglasses with UV protection.
- **Shade:** [between 10 a.m. and 2 p.m.](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/skin-cancer/prevent/how), when the sun is strongest.

## What I Avoid

I do not put essential oils on my skin. [Tea tree, ylang-ylang, lemongrass and clove oils](https://dermnetnz.org/topics/allergic-contact-dermatitis-to-essential-oils) are some of the most common causes of allergic skin reactions.

Bergamot oil is a special case. Unless it has been treated, it contains bergapten, which reacts with sunlight and [can cause a reaction like a severe sunburn](https://library.scconline.org/v021n10/49). On a sunny deck, that is the last thing I need.

## If You Only Do One Thing

Wear a broad-spectrum sunscreen with SPF 30 or higher, and put it on again every two hours.
